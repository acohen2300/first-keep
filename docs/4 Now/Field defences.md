What each was, what it did, where it is written down, and what it would be in the game. Sources opened Nov 3 unless marked "from memory". Nothing here is built; the choke and the ambush of preview 37 are the two tools the game has. [2026-11-03 12:34]

## 1. Stakes (the archer's stake) [2026-11-03 12:34]

- What: a pointed stake, six feet, driven into the ground at an angle in front of the archers so a horse that charges the line is impaled. Henry V ordered every archer to cut one before Agincourt (1415), after the Turks' stakes at Nicopolis (1396). Three readings of how they stood: a fence before the front rank (Burne), each archer's own stake making a belt several yards wide (Keegan), a fence with gaps and a looser band in front (Rogers). Henry VIII carried 5,000 stakes in wagons in 1513. Source: Wikipedia, "Archer's stake" (Strickland & Hardy 2005; Burne 1956; Keegan 1976; Rogers 2008; Cruikshank 1990), opened Nov 3. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- In the game: a shooter's hold. On a tap of Stakes, each picked shooter plants one at its place (a few seconds of work, a little wood); riders that charge a staked rank take a blow and stop at the stakes (vsMounted the other way round: the Front's 30% worse from riders becomes 30% better behind stakes). Pulled up when the shooters move (they are carried; Henry's were). Cheap to build on preview 37's shapes: one flag on the soldier, one check in the charge. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- My reading: yes. It is the ground's answer to the Charge, and it sits in the hold-and-tap grammar already there. [2026-11-03 12:34]

## 2. Caltrops [2026-11-03 12:34]

- What: four iron spikes joined so one always stands up (the Roman *tribulus*, Vegetius III, "Armed chariots and elephants": "rests on three and presents the fourth upright"); strewn on the field against chariots, horses, elephants and camels (the camel's soft foot worst). Quintus Curtius IV.13.36 has them at Gaugamela, 331 BC; the Romans strewed them against scythed chariots. No good evidence for the medieval battlefield uses the stories give them. Source: the Military Wiki's "Caltrop" (quoting Vegetius and Curtius), opened Nov 3; Wikipedia's own page not opened (not on the allowlist). [2026-11-03 12:34]
- In the game: a sown patch, drawn on the map like the choke (drag a line): a band of squares that slows and hurts anything mounted that crosses it (and a little anything on foot), unseen by the foe until the first is hurt; costs iron, and the Smithy must have it. The patch stays until picked up (villagers) or the game ends. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- My reading: yes, later. It needs iron on hand and a Smithy step, so it is an Iron-age tool; and it needs the fog to hide it, which the test field turns off. [2026-11-03 12:34]

## 3. Lilia and the other pits (Alesia) [2026-11-03 12:34]

- What: Caesar's three belts before the lines at Alesia, 52 BC (Gallic War VII.72–73, Perseus translation, opened Nov 3): trenches "twenty feet deep, with perpendicular sides" and two "fifteen feet broad, and of the same depth"; a rampart "twelve feet high" with turrets "eighty feet distant from one another"; the *cippi*, sharpened trunks set in a trench "five feet deep", "five rows in connection with, and intersecting each other"; the *lilia*, "pits three feet deep", a stake "of the thickness of a man's thigh" in each, projecting "not more than four inches", "eight rows ... three feet distant from each other"; and the spurs (*stimuli*), "stakes a foot long, with iron hooks attached to them", "at small intervals". [2026-11-03 12:34]
- In the game: the Pit already exists (BLD pit, a trap). A lily-field would be the Pit laid as a band, several rows, by the same drag as the choke; each pit takes one foe and is spent. It is a wall that costs no stone, for a side with villagers to dig. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- My reading: maybe. The Pit does most of it one at a time; the band is a convenience and a cost (eight rows of pits is a lot of digging seconds). Ask Albert whether the single Pit is enough. [2026-11-03 12:34]

## 4. Abatis [2026-11-03 12:34]

- What: felled trees laid toward the enemy with the branches sharpened, the trunks pinned; the oldest obstacle, Caesar's *cippi* are one form, and it was dug in to the Civil War and 1914. From memory, not opened: no page was opened for it today. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- In the game: the clear-and-burn of the camps (preview 38) already fells the trees under a camp; an abatis is the same felling kept as a wall: a band of squares that blocks walking like forest and takes blows like a palisade, made from the trees that stood there (free where there is forest, impossible where there is none). [2026-11-03 12:34]
- My reading: yes, as the camp's own outer work: a Camp set beside forest gets an abatis on its forest side for nothing. Small to build on the palisade code. [2026-11-03 12:34]

## 5. Chevaux de frise [2026-11-03 12:34]

- What: a log with sharpened stakes through it, portable, set to close a gap against horse; the name is "the Frisian horses", from the Frisians' wars of the 1500s–1600s, who had no cavalry of their own and closed their lines with them. From memory, not opened. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- In the game: the stakes of item 1 made into a thing the foot can carry: a piece, not a square, carried by two foot soldiers and set across a gap; the same block as the palisade but it can be picked up again. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- My reading: no for now. Stakes (1) and the palisade cover it; a movable wall piece is a new kind of thing to carry, drop and pick up, and the pick-up grammar does not exist. [2026-11-03 12:34]

## 6. Wagenburg (the wagon fort) [2026-11-03 12:34]

- What: wagons chained into a square with the horse inside; the Hussites' (1420s) crew of 18–21 to a wagon: 4–8 crossbowmen, 2 handgunners, 6–8 with pikes or flails, 2 with shields, 2 drivers; cannon first, then crossbow and gun at close range, then the sally. Hunyadi's *szekérvár* against the Ottomans (600 Bohemian handgunners at Varna, 1444); the Goths' *carrago* at Adrianople (Ammianus 31.7); the Voortrekkers' laager at Blood River, 1838 (about 460 against 10,000–15,000). Source: Wikipedia, "Wagon fort", opened Nov 3. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- In the game: the Camp wagon of preview 38 already drives out and becomes a square; a Wagenburg is that wagon, chained with others, as a fort you do not unpack: a ring of wagon pieces (hp, a shooter's step inside) that can be packed again and driven on. It needs several wagons (the Hussites' smallest forts had dozens) and a gunpowder age. [2026-11-03 12:34]
- My reading: later, Gunpowder age. It is the camps' step 3, not a field defence. [2026-11-03 12:34]
