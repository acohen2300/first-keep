# Sources retired, Nov 23, 2026

Nothing in the game used them. Each line: the code, why it went, then its full text as it stood.

- **N175**: measured on modern winter boots; the ice's grip (G8) uses N107, N18 and N178, and this paper's 0.08 and 0.20 are not its numbers.
  Hsu, J., Shaw, R., Novak, A., Li, Y., Ormerod, M., Newton, R., Dutta, T. & Fernie, G. (2016) Slip resistance of winter footwear on snow and ice measured using maximum achievable incline, Ergonomics 59(5):717-728, doi:10.1080/00140139.2015.1084051: Bench COF 0.08 wet ice, 0.20 dry ice, averaged over six winter boot styles (not 'bare boots')

- **N221**: a white oak's height on good forest sites; the tree's height (W25) is the coppiced wood by a village, from N258 and N259.
  Rogers, R. (1990), Quercus alba L., white oak, in Burns & Honkala, Silvics of North America vol. 2, Agriculture Handbook 654, USDA Forest Service (srs.fs.usda.gov/pubs/misc/ag_654/volume_2/quercus/alba.htm): often 24 to 30 m tall on good forest sites; site index 13 to 19 m at base age 50 (Missouri); no height-by-age curve on the page (the Forest Service's curves, Carmean, Hahn & Jacobs 1989, GTR NC-128, would not open: to open)

- **N289**: never opened (from memory); the woods' pace (D360) rests on N287, N292 and N294.
  US Army FM 21-18, Foot Marches (1990), the rates of march by ground: cross-country and wooded ground far slower than a road (from memory: about 4 km/h on roads, 2.4 cross-country, slower again in thick woods); to open for the woods' figure

- **N295**: Poisson-disk spacing: no code lays things this way any more.
  Poisson-disk sampling: Cook, R. L., Stochastic sampling in computer graphics (ACM Transactions on Graphics 5, 1986; blue noise, as the cones of the eye are laid); Bridson, R., Fast Poisson disk sampling in arbitrary dimensions (SIGGRAPH sketches, 2007; k = 30 tries a point). Plants that compete for light and water keep such a least distance apart. From memory, to open.

- **N296**: the spatial-hash primes: they are in no code or book any more (the computers' mix, D462, is its own).
  Teschner, M. et al., Optimized spatial hashing for collision detection of deformable objects (VMV 2003): the primes 73856093 and 19349663, so a piece's seed from its place never repeats along a row or a column. From memory, to open.

- **N297**: the varnish cel: no code draws it any more.
  Varnish: a clear coat fills a surface’s roughness, so less light scatters off the top and the colours under it read deeper (the conservator’s saturation effect); the clear coat cel, laid as a soft-light blend. Porter, T. and Duff, T., Compositing digital images (SIGGRAPH 1984): clear sheets laid over one another. Disney's multiplane camera (1937). From memory, to open.

- **N298**: Bridson's faster ring: no code lays things this way any more.
  Roberts, M., An improved version of Bridson's algorithm for Poisson disc sampling (Extreme Learning blog, 2019): candidates thrown on a thin ring just past r fill the plane as tightly with a few tries as Bridson's on the full annulus, several times faster. From memory, to open.

- **N444**: the BT.709 brightness weights: no code or book uses them any more.
  Recommendation ITU-R BT.709 (HDTV parameter values), derivation of the luminance signal: E'Y = 0.2126 E'R + 0.7152 E'G + 0.0722 E'B (item 4.2 in BT.709-3, 1998; item 3.2 in BT.709-6, the same weights). Opened Nov 21, 2026.
